01 // MARKET BASICS

HOW A MARKET MOVES

WHAT IS THE STOCK MARKET?

A marketplace where people buy and sell pieces of companies. Prices change as buyers and sellers react to information and demand.

WHAT IS A STOCK?

A stock is a small ownership share in a company. Buying means you own a small piece; selling means you pass that piece to another buyer.

BULL & BEAR MARKETS

Bull market: prices are generally rising. Bear market: prices are generally falling. Both describe a broad trend, not what every stock will do.

PRICE & MOVEMENT

Price is the latest amount someone paid. It moves when buyers are willing to pay more or sellers accept less. News, results, and expectations can all affect it.

02 // READING A CHART

PRICE, TIME, AND CANDLES

PRICE CHART EXAMPLE

WHAT A CHART SHOWS

A chart puts price on the vertical axis and time along the bottom, so you can see how price changed.

CANDLESTICK

Each candle is one selected time period. The body runs from open to close; wicks show the high and low.

O / H / L / C

Open: first price. High: highest. Low: lowest. Close: final price for that period.

BULLISH / BEARISH

A bullish candle closes above its open. A bearish candle closes below its open. One candle alone does not predict the next move.

TIMEFRAMES

1m, 30m, 1h, and 1D show one minute, 30 minutes, one hour, or one day per candle. Short timeframes show detail; longer ones help reveal the bigger picture.

03 // VOLUME

HOW MUCH WAS TRADED?

WHAT IS IT?

Volume is the number of shares or units traded during a period.

HIGH VS LOW

High volume can mean more interest in a move. Low volume can mean less participation.

HOW TO USE IT

Traders compare volume with price movement for context. It supports an observation; it does not confirm a guaranteed outcome.

04 // TRENDS

THE DIRECTION OF PRICE

UPTREND

Price generally makes higher highs and higher lows. It shows upward context, not a promise it will continue.

DOWNTREND

Price generally makes lower highs and lower lows. It shows downward context, not certainty.

SIDEWAYS / RANGE

Price moves mostly between two areas without a clear direction. Traders often wait for clearer context.

05 // SUPPORT & RESISTANCE

IMPORTANT PRICE AREAS

RESISTANCESUPPORT

SUPPORT

An area where a falling price has often slowed or bounced because buyers showed interest.

RESISTANCE

An area where a rising price has often slowed because sellers became more active.

BREAKOUT / BREAKDOWN

A breakout moves above resistance; a breakdown moves below support. These areas are zones, not exact guaranteed prices, and moves can fail.

06 // TECHNICAL INDICATORS

EXTRA CLUES, NOT ANSWERS

WHAT IS AN INDICATOR?

An indicator is a calculation shown on or below a chart. It helps organize price data, but it cannot guarantee future prices.

RSI

RSI shows how strongly price has been moving on a 0–100 scale. Below 30 is commonly called oversold and above 70 overbought. These levels do not guarantee a reversal.

MACD

MACD compares two moving averages to show momentum. A MACD-line and signal-line crossover can suggest momentum is changing, not guarantee a direction.

MOVING AVERAGE (MA)

An MA smooths past price data to show the general trend. Price above or below it can provide context, not an automatic buy or sell signal.

07 // CANDLESTICK PATTERNS

PATTERNS ARE CLUES

Patterns describe how candles look. Traders use their location, trend, and volume for context; no pattern guarantees the next move.

DOJI

Open and close are very close. It can show indecision.

HAMMER

Small body with a long lower wick. After a fall, it can show buyers pushing back.

SHOOTING STAR

Small body with a long upper wick. After a rise, it can show selling pressure.

BULLISH ENGULFING

A larger up candle covers a prior down candle. It can suggest buyers gained control.

BEARISH ENGULFING

A larger down candle covers a prior up candle. It can suggest sellers gained control.

08 // RISK MANAGEMENT

PROTECT THE PLAN

RISK VS REWARD

Before a trade, compare what you could lose with what you hope to gain. A good idea can still lose.

STOP LOSS

A planned exit level that limits loss if price moves against you. It may not always execute at the exact expected price.

POSITION SIZE

Decide how much of your virtual balance to allocate. Do not risk everything on one trade or one signal.

TRADING PLAN

Write down why you are entering, where you would exit, and how much you can risk. Indicators and signals are tools, not guarantees.
